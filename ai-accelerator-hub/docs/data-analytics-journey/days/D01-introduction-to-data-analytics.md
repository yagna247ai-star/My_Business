# Day 1 — What Is Data, and How Do We Use It?

> **Time:** 4 h 30 min · **Module:** M1 Fundamentals · **Shop:** Sunita Stores

## The problem
Sunita runs a grocery shop with two branches. Last month Sunita felt sales were slow. But Sunita has no report. Sunita only has a paper billing book, a WhatsApp group, and a bank statement. Sunita asks: "Were sales really slow? And why?"

## The big idea
**Data** is a record of something that happened. A bill is data. A bill says what was bought, how much it cost, and when. **Data analytics** means asking questions and using those records to find the answer.

Think of a cricket scorecard. Each ball is recorded. From those records, you can answer: "Who scored the most runs?" Shops work the same way.

## Words to know
- **Data**: a record of something that happened.
- **Analytics**: using data to answer a question.
- **Analyst**: the person who asks the question, finds the answer, and explains it.
- **Measurable question**: a question that can be answered with a number.

## Let's solve it
Sunita's question is too vague: "Were sales slow?" The word "slow" means different things to different people. So we make it measurable.

Compare three versions:
1. "Are we doing badly?" → Cannot be answered. What does "badly" mean?
2. "Did revenue drop last month?" → Can be answered with yes or no, but not why.
3. "How did weekly revenue change from September to October, by branch?" → Can be answered with a table of numbers.

Version 3 has three parts:
- **A measure** (revenue, the money earned)
- **A time period** (September to October, week by week)
- **A breakdown** (by branch)

## Your turn

2. Write 5 things you record in your own day. Example: a phone call, a payment.
3. Take one of Sunita's problems. Rewrite it as a measurable question with a measure, a time period, and a breakdown.

## Check yourself (trainer's answer key)
1. "Are we doing badly?" is weak because "badly" is not a number, and there is no time period.
2. Sunita has a WhatsApp group of daily totals, but it is not in one place. A good answer is any source Sunita has but does not use.
3. An analyst turns a business question into a checked answer and explains it.

**Answer key note:** Grocery records include bills (date, items, amount), stock receipts, supplier invoices, staff attendance, and daily totals. Good questions name a measure, a period, and a breakdown.

## Why this helps your job
Every analyst job starts with this skill: turning a vague worry into a question the data can answer.
