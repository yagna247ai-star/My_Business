# AI Accelerator Hub — The Simple Guide

Updated 23 November 2026

AI Accelerator Hub is a website where people learn data analytics and AI by doing real tasks, one small step at a time, and earn points as they go. This guide explains every part of it in plain words.

## 1. The big idea

Think of the Hub as a video game for learning data skills. Instead of fighting monsters, you solve real problems for shops, banks, schools and hospitals.

- You follow a **journey**: a 45-day path, like the levels of a game.
- Every day has small **steps**. Each step tells you why it matters, exactly what to click or type, and what you should see.
- You **prove** your work: you paste a result, upload a screenshot or share a link.
- You earn **points** for every step. Points help you climb the ranks.
- **AI coaches** help you when you are stuck. They give hints, not answers.
- Real people (**reviewers** and **mentors**) check your bigger projects and help you grow.

The Hub works in six languages: English, Hindi, Telugu, Tamil, Kannada and Malayalam.

## 2. Who uses it

Everyone who signs in has a **role**. Your role decides which menu items you see, like a key that opens some doors and not others.

| Role | Who they are | What they can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learner | A student learning data analytics | Follow a journey, do steps, earn points, ask AI coaches, join hackathons, publish to the showcase |
| Team Lead | A learner who leads a hackathon team | Everything a learner can do, plus manage their team |
| Reviewer | A person who checks proof | Score submitted proof against a fixed checklist (a rubric) |
| Instructor | A teacher | Review work, see class progress, write and check lesson content |
| Cohort Mentor | A guide for a group of learners | Review work, answer help requests, moderate the feed and showcase |
| Program Manager | Runs a programme | Manage groups and sessions, review content, see reports, moderate |
| Institution Admin | Runs the whole college or company account | Add people, change roles, manage groups, settings, moderation and reports |
| Finance | Looks after money | Plans, payments and invoices |
| Support | The help desk | Help people, moderate, read the audit log |
| Super Admin | The owner of the whole platform | Everything, everywhere, including publishing content |

If a menu item is missing for you, your role does not include it. Ask your Institution Admin.

## 3. Getting started

You can be learning in about five minutes.

1. Open the website and click **Register**.
2. Type your name, email, a password and the year you were born. If you are under 18, a parent or guardian must agree before some features open (see section 15).
3. Pick a **journey**. For example "AI-Powered Data Analytics", or a journey for one business area such as Banking, Healthcare or Education.
4. Some journeys have **tracks**: Easy, Intermediate, Hard or Critical. Pick the one that matches how much you already know. Hard and Critical add Databricks, PySpark and fixing an old ("legacy") data pipeline.
5. Sign in. **Mission Control** is your home page: your next step, your points and your streak.
6. To change the language, open **Settings** (or the language menu) and pick one of the six languages. The whole site, your lessons and your activities switch to it.

Tip: lessons are translated by AI. Code, SQL and Excel formulas always stay in English, because computers only understand them that way.

## 4. How learning is organised

Learning is packed like a set of boxes inside boxes: a journey holds levels, a level holds days and activities, and a day holds steps.

| Box | How many | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Journey | 1 | Your whole course: 45 days, from "what is data?" to a final project |
| Level | 9 per journey | One topic, such as SQL, Python or Power BI. Shown as tabs at the top of the Journey page |
| Day | about 5 per level | One lesson plus its guided steps |
| Activity | 6 per level | Bigger tasks: a briefing, a quiz, a hands-on build, proof and a final challenge |
| Step | 2–5 per day | One small job with a check and points |

**The level page** shows, from top to bottom: the level tabs (done ✓, open, or locked), "Words you will see", a ring showing how many activities you finished, **Your days**, **Your activities**, a "Get ready" checklist (things to install first) and class times if your group has live sessions.

**The activity page** shows one activity at a time: what you do here, what you leave with, why it matters, the task itself, "Was this useful?", and buttons for the previous and next activity. On the right, the **AI coach** waits to help (section 10).

Finished items get a big green ✓, so you always know what is done.

## 5. Guided steps: one small job at a time

Every day is split into small steps, like a recipe. At the top of each day a blue banner says how many steps there are, how many points you can earn and how long it takes, with a **Start step 1 ↓** button.

Each step has five parts:

1. **Why**: what this step is for, explained with an everyday example.
2. **Do this**: numbered actions. Commands, SQL, Python and formulas sit in dark boxes with a **Copy** button, so you never have to retype them. Key presses look like keyboard keys, such as Ctrl + Enter.
3. **You should see**: an example of what the screen shows when it worked.
4. **Stuck?**: the most common problems and how to fix each one.
5. **Check**: you prove the step is done, then you get its points.

There are four kinds of check:

| Check | What you do | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tick | Tick "I did this" | 2 |
| Answer | Write a short answer in your own words | 3 |
| Paste | Paste the result your computer showed you | 5 |
| Screenshot | Upload a picture of your own screen | 5 |

**Windows or Mac?** When the steps are different on each computer, a switch at the top lets you choose. The Hub remembers your choice.

**No downloads needed.** SQL days start with a script you copy that builds the practice tables for you. Python days give you a small program that makes its own practice file.

Answers are checked for **quality, not length**. There is no word counter: a short, correct answer passes and a long, off-topic one does not.

## 6. The six activities in every level

Besides the days, every level has six bigger activities. They go in this order:

1. **Mission Briefing**: read the business problem, like a detective reading the case file, then answer one question to show you understood it.
2. **Learn**: short lessons and a quiz. You need about 60% to pass, and you can try again.
3. **Evidence**: either a **Dataset Check** (upload your CSV or Excel file and the Hub checks its columns and values) or a **Screenshot** of your work.
4. **Build**: use an AI tool to help you make something, then write what you asked it, what it gave you and what you changed.
5. **Submit Proof**: share a link to your work and write what you did, what surprised you and what you learned. A reviewer scores it.
6. **Boss Challenge**: real situations where you pick the right move and say why. You get 3 tries.

After each activity you can tap thumbs up or thumbs down under "Was this useful?". The team reads these to improve the lessons.

## 7. Moving on freely

You are never stuck behind a locked door. The rule is simple: finish **or skip** everything in a level, and the next level opens.

- **Skip** any step, day or activity with the Skip button. You move on straight away; that item just earns 0 points for now.
- **Come back later** and do a skipped item to earn its points. The next level stays open.
- **Review never blocks you.** Your proof may wait for a reviewer, but you keep learning while you wait.
- When a level is complete, the Hub tells you your total, for example: "You earned 180 of 240 points in this level."

Skipping is allowed, but your total points and your ranks show how much you really did.

## 8. Points, streaks, ranks and badges

Points (also called **XP**) are how the Hub says "well done". You earn them for steps, days, activities and challenges.

| Feature | Where | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| XP | Top bar | Your total points. Each point is earned once; doing a step twice does not pay twice |
| Streak | Top bar | How many days in a row you learned. Miss a day and you can win it back with a short recovery quiz |
| Achievements | Achievements page | Badges for milestones, such as finishing a level |
| Ranks | Ranks page | Your title grows as your points grow |
| Standings | Standings page | A leaderboard of learners in your institution |
| Area crowns | Area crowns page | The top learner in each topic area wears the crown |

Every point is written into a tamper-proof list (a "ledger"), so nobody can quietly change scores.

## 9. Proof: what counts and what gets refused

Proof must be **your own work**, and it must **open for everyone**. If something is wrong, the Hub refuses it at once and tells you how to fix it.

**Links are refused when they are:**

- not a place where work is kept (good places: GitHub, Google Drive or Docs, OneDrive, Databricks, Power BI, Colab, Kaggle, Notion, or a Loom or YouTube walkthrough);
- social media, a search page, a short link (bit.ly), a ChatGPT chat or Wikipedia;
- a site's home page or a GitHub profile instead of your own file or project;
- a link another learner already handed in;
- **private**: if other people would see "Request access", it is refused. Fix it in Google Drive with Share → General access → "Anyone with the link" → Viewer, or make your GitHub repository public.

**Screenshots are refused when they are:**

- too small to read;
- the same picture another learner uploaded;
- a picture you already used for a different step;
- clearly not your work, such as a photo, a meme or a blank screen (when the AI checker is switched on).

**Answers are refused when they are** off topic, nonsense, or contain phone numbers, email addresses or ID numbers.

A slow internet connection never blocks you: if the Hub cannot check a link, it lets it through.

## 10. AI coaches: help without the answers

AI coaches are like a patient tutor sitting next to you. They explain ideas and give hints, but they **never do your graded work** for you.

- **Beside every activity**: the right side of each activity page has "Ask the AI coach". It already knows that activity, so you can ask straight away, or tap a starter question such as "Explain this problem in simple words".
- **AI Coaches page**: choose a coach for a bigger chat, for example the Learning Coach, the Project Mentor or the Career Mentor.
- **Stuck helper**: on a level, "I'm stuck" gives up to three hints, then sends your question to a real mentor.
- **Credits**: each question uses one credit. Your plan gives you a number of credits, and the count shows under the chat.
- **Privacy**: before your question leaves the Hub, phone numbers, emails and secrets are removed automatically.

AI can be wrong. Always check its numbers, just as the lessons teach you to.

## 11. Community: learning together

Only people from your own institution can see these pages.

**Feed**: like a class notice board. Post questions, share wins, comment and react. You can report a post that breaks the rules, or block someone; moderators check reports.

**Showcase**: a gallery of the best projects.

1. A reviewer must approve your proof first.
2. Open Showcase, choose that approved work, give it a title and a short description, and click **Publish to showcase**. It appears straight away.
3. Your project link must open for everyone; private links are refused with steps to fix them.
4. Your own project has a **Remove** button. Other people's projects have a **Report** button.
5. Moderators can **feature** a great project (a star at the top), **hide** one with a reason (only the author still sees it, with the reason) or **remove** it with a reason.

**Hackathons**: team competitions with a deadline. Form or join a team, submit your entry, and review other teams' work (peer review). Judges decide the winners.

**Open-source Library**: free, checked learning resources and code projects to explore, each with its licence and difficulty.

## 12. Agent Lab and career tools

**Agent Lab**: a guided workshop for setting up an AI coding helper (an "agent", such as Claude Code) on your own computer. Pick Windows, Mac or Linux and follow the cards: why, what to do, what you should see, and what to do if it fails. Paste the result to prove it worked; a reviewer checks it under "Agent Lab Proofs". If you get stuck, "Stuck on this step?" shows known errors and their fixes.

**My Path**: a few questions about you (student, working person, business owner…) so the Hub can suggest the right journey and give the AI coaches the right tone.

**Career**: build your profile from your approved work.

**Talent**: companies in the Hub post jobs and internships. They can only find you if you agree, and you can apply, withdraw and see where your application stands. Learners under 18 cannot apply.

## 13. For reviewers and mentors

Reviewers and mentors are the referees and coaches of the Hub.

- **Review Queue**: proof waiting to be checked, oldest first. Open one, score it against the rubric (a fixed checklist, so everyone is judged the same way) and approve it or ask for changes. An automatic first check runs before you see it.
- **Appeals**: a learner who disagrees with a score can appeal to a mentor, who decides.
- **Agent Lab Proofs**: check learners' setup results from the Agent Lab.
- **Help Requests**: questions the AI stuck helper could not solve. Answer them so the learner can carry on.
- **360° evaluation** (Education journeys): a learner's score is built from four parts: academics 45%, attendance 20%, cultural activities 15% and sports 20%.
- **Moderation**: mentors can also check reported feed posts and showcase projects.

## 14. For admins and the Super Admin

Admins are like the school office: they set things up so everyone else can learn. These pages sit under **Program** in the menu.

| Page | What you do there | Who sees it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cohort Analytics | See how groups are doing: progress, points, who is falling behind | Admins, Program Managers, Instructors |
| People | Invite people, change roles, deactivate accounts | Institution Admin, Super Admin |
| Cohorts & Sessions | Make groups of learners and plan live class times | Admins, Program Managers |
| Content review | Read every journey exactly as learners see it, including the answers, leave suggestions and see thumbs-up/down counts | Super Admin, Institution Admin, Program Manager, Instructor |
| Publishing levels | Publish or unpublish a level, or publish all of a journey's drafts | Super Admin only |
| Journey Builder | Create and change journeys | Admins with content rights |
| Hackathon Builder | Create hackathons and set their dates and rules | Admins with content rights |
| Moderation | Check reported feed posts; hide or keep them | Super Admin, Institution Admin, Program Manager, Mentor, Support |
| Showcase | Feature, hide (with a reason), show again, remove (with a reason) and answer reports | Super Admin, Institution Admin, Program Manager, Mentor, Support |
| Audit Log | A list of every important action: who, what and when | Admins, Support, Finance |
| Plans & Billing | Plans, payments and invoices | Finance, Admins |

Every admin action is written to the **Audit Log**, so it can always be checked later.

**Language fill**: the Hub translates its own buttons and labels into the five Indian languages every night, using whichever AI service is connected.

## 15. Safety and privacy

The Hub is built to keep learners, especially young ones, safe.

- **Under 18**: a parent or guardian must agree before a young learner can publish to the showcase, and young learners can never apply for jobs. AI coaches follow extra safety rules for them.
- **Your institution only**: the feed, showcase and standings are visible only to people from your own college or company.
- **Personal data is protected**: names, phone numbers and ID numbers are removed before anything is sent to an AI, and answers with phone numbers or emails are refused.
- **Files are checked**: every upload is scanned, must be a real image or file of the type it claims, and can only be opened by the people allowed to see it.
- **Nothing is secret from the record**: grades, points and admin actions are logged, so mistakes can be traced and fixed.
- **Talent is opt-in**: companies only see you if you say yes.
