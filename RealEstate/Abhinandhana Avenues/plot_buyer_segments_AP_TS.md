# Open Plot (Vacant Residential Land) Buyer Research — Andhra Pradesh & Telangana

**Prepared for:** Abhinandhana Avenues Private Limited (open plots with EMI options, AP + Telangana)
**Date:** 5 August 2026
**Method:** Public web research (news, industry commentary, portal listings, academic papers, government/IGRS data). All data points flagged **[CONFIRMED]** (official source or multiple independent sources) vs **[ESTIMATED]** (single source, listing-derived, or inferred).

---

## Executive Summary (Key Takeaways)

1. **NRI money is the single biggest structural driver** of plot demand in AP/TS. AP received **~USD 4+ bn remittances in FY24** (AP+TS together > USD 12 bn from US/UK/other developed markets) — reported figures [CONFIRMED]. Gulf (GCC) still accounts for ~55.8% of India's total remittance inflow [CONFIRMED, academic source].
2. **FEMA makes open plots the ONLY legal real-estate vehicle for NRIs** in most of AP/TS: NRIs/OCIs are **barred from buying agricultural land, plantation land, and farmhouses** — so an approved residential plot is the default channel for NRI capital [CONFIRMED, MEA/FEMA guidance + multiple sources].
3. **EMI is standard practice, not a differentiator.** Developer-financed plot EMI schemes (10–20% down, 12–60 months, "0% interest" for ≤12 months) are ubiquitous across Kurnool, Anantapur, Bhongir, Hyderabad periphery. Low conversion is therefore more likely a **trust/approvals/follow-up problem than a financing-availability problem** [ESTIMATED inference from market practice].
4. **Bank land loans are NOT the bottleneck either**: plot loans run at 75–80% LTV, ~8.35–9.5% p.a. interest, 10–20 year tenure — but only for **approved layouts (DTCP/HMDA/CRDA/VMRDA/KUDA)**. Unapproved land gets no finance [CONFIRMED].
5. **Budget reality**: a 100–200 sq yd plot in the target markets costs roughly **₹6–30 lakh** (Kurnool, Kadapa, Nellore, Anantapur, Hyderabad outer periphery) up to **₹15–60 lakh** (Vijayawada/Guntur outskirts, Vizag, Tirupati). EMI schemes are engineered precisely for this ticket size [CONFIRMED listings + ESTIMATED ranges].

## 1. Buyer Segment Profiles

### a) Gulf NRIs (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar)
*Strong presence: Kurnool, Anantapur, Kadapa (YSR), Chittoor, Nellore belts + coastal AP*

**Typical motivation — [CONFIRMED, multi-source]:**
- Predominantly **investment + family security** (send money home; buy land as savings vehicle while working in GCC). Academic studies of Gulf migrants from Kadapa (YSR district) show remittances used for property/land purchase and housing improvement; the IJSSER 2024 study of Gulf migrant households documents savings channelled into real estate and land [CONFIRMED, ijcrt.org/papers/IJCRT1134730.pdf; ijsser.org/2024files/ijsser_09__128.pdf].
- GCC = ~55.8% of India's remittance inflow; Gulf NRIs are reported to be actively shifting into Indian real estate, including as prices cool (ET, Jun 2025; Arabian Business, Feb 2024) [CONFIRMED].
- Since NRIs **cannot buy agricultural land** under FEMA, the open residential plot (approved layout) is the mainstream instrument [CONFIRMED].
- Anantapur/Kurnool marketing explicitly targets NRIs ("NRIs can invest securely in Kurnool plots") [CONFIRMED, ananthaprojects.com blog].

**Typical budget range — [ESTIMATED]:**
- ₹5–25 lakh per plot (100–200 sq yd), matching Kurnool (₹6,000–16,000/sq yd) and Anantapur EMI-plot ticket sizes. Premium plots near district HQ/airport corridors go higher (₹25–50 lakh). Gulf NRI ticket sizes are smaller than US/UK NRI tickets.
- Down-payment-style EMI schemes (10–20% down) fit Gulf worker cash-flow (monthly salary remittance).

**Decision timeline — [ESTIMATED]:**
- Purchase decisions cluster around **annual home visits** (summer, Eid/Onam-Sankranti season) — typically 1–3 months of active consideration, often initiated remotely by WhatsApp with a family member (parent/sibling) doing site visits and registration.
- Token/booking often paid by family member on NRI's behalf via inward remittance (FEMA-compliant channel).

**Key concerns — [CONFIRMED + ESTIMATED]:**
- **Title clarity, encumbrance, Dharani/land-record verification** — top concern for remote buyers (sources: NRI legal guides, bhoovix, video guides on buying AP land) [CONFIRMED].
- **Approval status** (DTCP/HMDA/CRDA/KUDA-approved layouts) — because unapproved plots cannot be bank-financed and carry demolition/penalty risk [CONFIRMED, bigproperty.in].
- **Fraud risk / unauthorised brokers**; need for power-of-attorney and remote management [ESTIMATED/common industry knowledge].
- **Resale liquidity** — can they exit in 5–10 years when they return? Gulf NRIs often buy with a "return-to-India" plan [ESTIMATED].
- **Gulf job insecurity / visa risks** — remittance interruptions hit EMI commitments [ESTIMATED].

**Where they typically buy — [CONFIRMED via listings/industry presence]:**
- Home districts: Kurnool (Ballari Road, DRDO/Airport/Steel Plant corridors — e.g., Skandhanshi projects marketed with 12-month EMI), Anantapur (incl. Guntakal, Kodar), Kadapa, Chittoor/Tirupati belt, Nellore, and coastal AP towns. Dedicated "Estate Agents For NRI" directories exist in Krishna, Kadapa, West Godavari — evidence of an NRI-targeted sales ecosystem [CONFIRMED, Justdial listings].

### b) US / UK / Europe NRIs
*Strong presence: coastal AP — Krishna, Guntur, East Godavari, West Godavari (+ Vizianagaram/Vizag diaspora)*

**Typical motivation — [CONFIRMED, multi-source]:**
- **Retirement planning + emotional/rootedness anchor + investment**: The Hindu (May 2025), Mint (Dec 2022) and Omaxe (Oct 2025) all document NRI buying for cultural connection, retirement nest, and high returns; GetBelong notes many buy for aging parents/siblings (family obligation) [CONFIRMED].
- Academic literature documents the coastal-Andhra ↔ US transnational corridor (SAGE 2018 paper on high-skilled migrant mobility; Carol Upadhya's work on Coastal Andhra's transnational political economy) [CONFIRMED].
- NRI share of Indian property purchases has risen from 7–10% (a decade ago) to an estimated **18–20% in 2025** [CONFIRMED, industry report via LinkedIn news].
- East Godavari is being called "Andhra's next real estate hub" (Rajahmundry, Kakinada, Amalapuram) with NRI-led demand; developers explicitly court NRI buyers (e.g., Iresh Homes Kakinada) [CONFIRMED, rprealtyplus.com Apr 2026; ireshhomesindia.com Jun 2026].

**Typical budget range — [ESTIMATED]:**
- **₹15–60 lakh per plot**; these buyers are more likely to take 150–300 sq yd gated/villa plots near district HQs (Vijayawada/Guntur outskirts ₹25,000+/sq yd; Vizag ₹15,000–20,000+/sq yd; Rajahmundry/Kakinada premium plots). Dollar incomes make rupee assets "cheap", especially when INR depreciates [ESTIMATED].

**Decision timeline — [ESTIMATED]:**
- Longest diligence of all segments: **3–12 months**. Remote due-diligence via relatives, lawyers, online records (Dharani/IGRS), then purchase on next India trip. US NRIs are the most document-sensitive buyers.

**Key concerns — [CONFIRMED + ESTIMATED]:**
- Title perfection & litigation-free property (top priority; will pay premium for "clean title" branded plots — Business Standard notes cleaner titles attract buyers even at premium prices) [CONFIRMED].
- **US tax reporting & inheritance planning**; FEMA compliance on funding route; PoA/registration formalities for absent buyers [ESTIMATED/common NRI-advisor knowledge].
- **Property management while abroad** (maintenance, encroachment, boundary disputes, municipal charges) — Assetly and similar NRI property-management platforms exist precisely for this [CONFIRMED, assetlyhq.com].
- Capital-gains and circle-rate exposure on eventual sale [CONFIRMED, assetlyhq].

**Where they typically buy — [CONFIRMED]:**
- Krishna (Vijayawada), Guntur (incl. Amaravati capital region belt: Mangalagiri, Kaza, Tadikonda), East/West Godavari (Rajahmundry, Kakinada, Amalapuram, Bhimavaram), Visakhapatnam. SBI runs dedicated NRI branches in East Godavari — evidence of high NRI banking/property activity [CONFIRMED].

### c) Salaried professionals (Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Chennai) buying plots in home districts / hometowns

**Typical motivation — [CONFIRMED + ESTIMATED]:**
- **Retirement + parents' security + slow appreciation play**: professionals buy land as a low-risk, no-maintenance asset; land/gold are culturally considered "safe" stores of value (Reddit r/personalfinanceindia threads; khaliplot.com "plots are the safest real-estate investment") [CONFIRMED].
- Buying in the **hometown/home district** (often the parents' village or the nearest district HQ town) so parents can monitor the asset and a future house can serve retirement [ESTIMATED — consistent with multiple sources on NRI/professional behaviour].
- Working-class/professional demand visible in Hyderabad sub-urban markets (e.g., Reddit r/hyderabadrealestate: "buy a 100–120 sq yd plot and construct" plans) [CONFIRMED, Reddit thread].

**Typical budget range — [ESTIMATED]:**
- **₹6–30 lakh** for 100–200 sq yd in home towns; Hyderabad-periphery option (Chevella ~₹7,500–9,000/sq yd; Sangareddy ~₹2,000–17,000/sq yd listings) means a 100 sq yd plot ≈ **₹7–17 lakh** [CONFIRMED listing ranges].
- Typically self-funded or 75–80% bank plot loan (they have the salary slips banks need) [CONFIRMED, bankbazaar].

**Decision timeline — [ESTIMATED]:**
- **2–8 months**; decision often tied to appraisal/savings milestone, festival season, or a parent's push. EMI schemes ("₹10,000–20,000/month") are designed for exactly this segment.

**Key concerns — [ESTIMATED]:**
- EMI affordability vs existing home-loan EMI (their own or parents'); many already carry housing loans.
- Location quality (schools, hospitals, roads) for eventual parents'/retirement use.
- Appreciation rate and resale exit if they never build.
- Documentation they can verify remotely (they're educated buyers — will check Dharani/IGRS online).

**Where they typically buy — [ESTIMATED]:**
- Their own home districts across AP/TS (Rayalaseema + coastal belts + Telangana districts); Bengaluru/Chennai professionals disproportionately buy in coastal AP (Chittoor, Nellore, Guntur belts — closer to their workplaces).

### d) Small investors / local businessmen (buying for appreciation)

**Typical motivation — [CONFIRMED + ESTIMATED]:**
- **Pure appreciation/speculation**: buy at early-stage layout prices near announced infrastructure (airport, DRDO, steel plant, pharma city, ORR, capital region), hold 3–7 years, flip [ESTIMATED — visible in Kurnool DRDO/Airport marketing (Skandhanshi) and East-Hyderabad HMDA growth-area promos].
- Land is favoured by local businesspeople as a **parking of surplus cash with low paperwork**; black/white component common in land deals [ESTIMATED, industry knowledge].
- Plot purchases are a stated "next big investment" theme in Telangana (Landwey: agri-land conversions, Chevella plot buying surge) [CONFIRMED, landwey.in].

**Typical budget range — [ESTIMATED]:**
- **₹10–60 lakh per transaction**; often buys **2–4 plots at once** or large-plot-then-subdivide. Ticket sizes in peripheral growth corridors (₹2,000–9,000/sq yd guidance-rate zones) allow multiple-unit plays [CONFIRMED guidance data + ESTIMATED behaviour].

**Decision timeline — [ESTIMATED]:**
- **Fastest segment: 2–8 weeks.** Moves on price/news triggers; less sensitive to EMI (many pay lump-sum). They respond to "limited offer", price-per-sq-yd comparisons vs guidance value, and appreciation projections.

**Key concerns — [ESTIMATED]:**
- Price vs **guidance/circle rate** (avoid overpaying; register at fair value to avoid deemed-income tax).
- **Liquidity/exit** — can they resell within their horizon?
- Legal cleanliness (they will flip — title defects kill resale).
- Infrastructure timeline credibility (announced vs sanctioned vs constructed).

**Where they typically buy — [ESTIMATED]:**
- Growth corridors: Hyderabad periphery (ORR arc, Chevella, Shadnagar, Sangareddy, Bhongir), Amaravati capital region (Guntur–Vijayawada belt), Vizag growth corridors (Bhogapuram airport zone), Kurnool (DRDO/airport/steel plant), Tirupati/Chittoor.

### e) Farmers who sold agricultural land (reinvesting in plots) + end-users (buy a plot to build later)

**Typical motivation — [CONFIRMED + ESTIMATED]:**
- **Farmers: asset conversion, not de-risk.** After selling (or donating via land-pooling) farmland, proceeds are redeployed into **approved open plots** for capital preservation and appreciation. In rural areas, agricultural land is typically a **non-capital asset (tax-free sale)** — reinvesting in plots converts tax-free cash into a growth asset while keeping land-holding culture [ESTIMATED — general income-tax law; verify with CA].
- **Land-pooling precedent (Amaravati):** under the APCRDA Land Pooling Scheme, farmers contributed ~34,000 acres and received compensation + returnable developed plots (≈50% of land back as developed plots) — i.e., farmers in this belt are already "plot-holders" by experience [CONFIRMED, World Bank RPF doc; LinkedIn commentary].
- **End-users:** purchase a plot now at a locked price to **build a home in 3–10 years** (children's education timing, retirement, or marriage); land as a hedge against construction-cost inflation [ESTIMATED/common behaviour; khaliplot.com supports plot-as-stable-investment framing].

**Typical budget range — [ESTIMATED]:**
- Farmers: **₹5–50 lakh** depending on land-sale size (AP agri-land listings span ₹5 lakh to ₹2 cr+; typical small-holding sale = ₹10–40 lakh). End-users: **₹6–25 lakh** for 100–200 sq yd in tier-2/3 towns.
- End-users are the core **EMI-scheme audience** (₹10,000–20,000/month plans across Kurnool/Anantapur/Bhongir) [CONFIRMED listings].

**Decision timeline — [ESTIMATED]:**
- Farmers: **fast (1–3 months)** after the land sale completes — cash needs to be deployed quickly. End-users: **slowest (6–24 months)**; they save toward the plot, compare 3–5 projects, and often buy only after a site visit with family.

**Key concerns — [ESTIMATED]:**
- Farmers: safe title (they understand land fraud), **Dharani mutation**, avoiding reinvestment into another agricultural plot (redundant exposure), income-tax treatment of the sale, and EMI-free lump-sum options.
- End-users: monthly EMI capacity, total cost (plot + registration + future construction), approval status (will the bank finance it / is it safe to build), neighbourhood development pace, possession/registration timing.

**Where they typically buy — [ESTIMATED]:**
- Near their own village/town (familiar geography, family oversight): Rayalaseema districts, Guntur–Krishna belt, Godavari districts, Telangana districts around Hyderabad. End-users buy within 10–25 km of their workplace or their parents' home.

## 2. EMI / Financing Behaviour

### 2.1 Bank land-loan (plot loan) norms in India — [CONFIRMED, bankbazaar + bigproperty + Yesh Developers]

| Parameter | Plot/Land loan |
|---|---|
| **LTV (loan-to-value)** | Typically **75–80%** of plot value; can go up to 90% only for plot values < ₹75 lakh at some lenders' discretion. Practically, many lenders demand **min. 30% own contribution** (i.e., ~70% LTV). **[CONFIRMED]** |
| **Interest rate (2026)** | ~**8.35%–9.50% p.a.** (SBI 9.50%+; HDFC 8.75%+; Kotak 8.75%; IDFC First 8.85%; ICICI 9.00%; BoM 8.35%) **[CONFIRMED]** |
| **Tenure** | Up to **15 years** (some lenders 10–20 years); vs 30 years for home loans **[CONFIRMED]** |
| **Eligible collateral** | Only **approved layouts** — DTCP/HMDA/CRDA/VMRDA/KUDA approvals; **unapproved/agricultural land is generally not financed** **[CONFIRMED, bigproperty.in]** |
| **Tax benefit** | **None on the plot loan itself**; deductions (Sec 80C/24) unlock only after house construction on the plot (and ideally converting to a home loan) **[CONFIRMED]** |
| **NRI eligibility** | Yes — NRIs can take plot loans for **residential** plots **[CONFIRMED]** |
| **RBI housing-LTV backdrop** | Home loans: up to 90% (≤₹30L), 80% (₹30–75L), 75% (>₹75L) — plot loans are set by bank board policy within similar ceilings **[CONFIRMED, RBI/Tata Capital/99acres]** |

**Implication:** Bank financing exists and is cheap enough — but it **only works on approved layouts**. For an unapproved or "will be approved" plot, the developer EMI scheme is the only credit channel, which is exactly why EMI schemes dominate in tier-2/3 plot markets.

### 2.2 How developer-financed plot EMI schemes work in AP/TS — [CONFIRMED, developer/portal listings]

Observed structures (multiple developers, Kurnool / Anantapur / Bhongir / Hyderabad periphery):

- **Down payment: 10–20%** of plot value ("start with as low as 10–20%") **[CONFIRMED, dreamplots.in Anantapur]**.
- **Tenure: 12–60 months** (common: 12 / 24 / 36 months; some up to 60) **[CONFIRMED, dreamplots.in; OLX "EMI 12/24/36 MONTHS GATED TOWNSHIP"]**.
- **"0% interest" claims** — typically only for **short-tenure plans (≤12 months)**; longer tenures carry implicit pricing (inflated per-sq-yd rate vs cash price) **[CONFIRMED: "Select projects offer 0% interest on short-term installment plans up to 12 months" — dreamplots.in; ESTIMATED: longer-tenure pricing premium not publicly itemised]**.
- **Real examples:**
  - Bhongir (Telangana), DTCP-approved 150 sq yd: **booking ₹80,000 + EMI ₹14,500/month** **[CONFIRMED, 24acres.in]**.
  - Anantapur (SKU College area): **2.75 cents, ₹5.5 lakh, 10% spot registration, EMI ₹10,000/month** **[CONFIRMED, Facebook listing]**.
  - Kurnool (Skandhanshi DIVI, KUDA-approved): **₹20,000/month EMI**; Golden Oaks: **12-month EMI**, marketed against DRDO/Airport/Steel Plant/Drone Hub projects **[CONFIRMED, Instagram/Facebook]**.
  - Telugu YouTube ads: "100 sq yd plot with ₹12,000/month EMI" (≈₹12–14 lakh all-in) **[CONFIRMED, YouTube ad]**.
- **Registry/sale-deed timing varies**: spot registration with 10% down (risk to buyer), or deed after full payment (risk to buyer if developer defaults) — **terms are the #1 source of buyer distrust** [ESTIMATED].

### 2.3 Buyer reaction: do EMIs increase or decrease conversion for plots?

**Verdict: EMIs are a conversion *enabler* for cash-constrained segments (salaried, end-users, Gulf workers) but do not by themselves close deals — and mis-sold "0% EMI" schemes can *depress* conversion by raising distrust.**

Evidence:
- **National BNPL/flexible-payment trend**: "Realty developers roll out flexible payment plans amid slow sales… now being deployed widely **to boost conversions**, especially where buyers are wary of large upfront payments" (Rediff, May 2026; Kalpataru 10:90 scheme); "developers aggressively reviving BNPL schemes to boost sales" (Hindustan Times, Jul 2026) **[CONFIRMED]**.
- Flexible plans are better suited to **end-users** managing instalments alongside existing EMIs/SIPs (Rediff, May 2026) **[CONFIRMED]**.
- **Risks**: subvention/EMI schemes have a history of developer default, leaving buyers with accumulated interest — savvy buyers (incl. NRIs) discount EMI offers accordingly (Rediff, May 2026) **[CONFIRMED]**.
- **For plots specifically**: no public study found quantifying conversion uplift in AP/TS. Industry practice (ubiquity of EMI schemes in Kurnool/Anantapur/Bhongir) indicates EMI is now **table-stakes** — its presence rarely differentiates, its absence in same-priced competitors does **[ESTIMATED inference]**.
- **Negative side-effects to watch**: EMI-heavy marketing attracts low-intent leads (price shoppers) → low site-visit-to-booking conversion; EMI default risk on 24–60 month plans hits developer cash flow; "0% interest" claims invite consumer-law scrutiny when the underlying rate is embedded in the price **[ESTIMATED]**.
