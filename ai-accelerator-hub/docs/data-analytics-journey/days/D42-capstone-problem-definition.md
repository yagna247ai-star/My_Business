# Day 42 — Choosing Your Big Project and Defining the Problem

> **Time:** 4 h 30 min · **Module:** M9 Capstone · **Shop:** Choose one of the three capstones

## The problem
You have four days to finish one big project. It must be a real problem for a real-style shop, clinic, or workshop. Before you write any code, you must do one thing well: turn a vague request into a clear question.

A vague request sounds like: "Help us understand our sales." A clear question sounds like: "Which five products earned the most in each branch in the last quarter, and how did that change from the quarter before?"

## The big idea
A **problem definition** is a short page with six parts:
1. **Question**: one measurable question.
2. **Audience**: who will read the answer.
3. **Decision**: what they will do with it.
4. **Data**: which files or tables you will use.
5. **Success**: how you will know your answer is good.
6. **Out of scope**: what you will leave out, so the project stays small.

Think of planning a school trip. "Go somewhere fun" is not a plan. "Go to the science museum, by bus, on Friday, for 60 students, with lunch included" is a plan.

## The three capstones
| Capstone | Shop | Your question could be |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Retail Sales** | Sunita Stores | Which products sell best? How do months and branches compare? |
| **2. HR Employees** | Pinnacle Tools | Who is leaving, and in which departments? What are the average pay numbers? |
| **3. Healthcare** | Bright Smile Dental Clinic | Which visit patterns are common? Can we trust the data? |

Pick the one that interests you most. Choose the one you can finish in four days.

## Words to know
- **Scope**: the list of things your project will do.
- **Audience**: the person who reads your answer.
- **Success measure**: how you will know it worked.

## Let's solve it
Take Capstone 1. A weak plan: "Analyse sales." A strong plan:
- **Question**: Which product is top in each branch, each month, for the last three months?
- **Audience**: Sunita, the owner.
- **Decision**: Which products to order more of next month.
- **Data**: the sales file and the product list.
- **Success**: a table and one chart, with each number checked against the raw file.
- **Out of scope**: customer names, pricing changes, and forecasts.

## Your turn
1. Read the three capstone briefs. Pick one.
2. Write the one-page problem definition with all six parts.
3. List your data sources. For each, write the period it covers, the number of rows, and one known gap.
4. Write three questions the owner would ask you first. Answer each one.

**Hand in:** the one-page definition, your data table, and the three questions with answers.

## Check yourself (trainer's answer key)
1. A topic is "sales". A problem definition is a question with a measure, a time, and a decision behind it.
2. The decision shows why the answer matters.
3. Name one thing you left out. Say why, so the project stays doable in four days.

## Why this helps your job
Turning a vague request into a clear question is the most valued skill in analyst interviews.
