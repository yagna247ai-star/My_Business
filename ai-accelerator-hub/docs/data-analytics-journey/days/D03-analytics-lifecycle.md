# Day 3 — The Five Steps From Messy Records to a Clear Answer

> **Time:** 4 h 30 min · **Module:** M1 Fundamentals · **Shop:** Ganesh Micro-Credit Co-op

## The problem
The co-op lends small loans to shop owners. Each month, the board gets a report of "loans that are late". Two months in a row, the report was wrong. Some members who had paid were listed as late. Nobody knows where it went wrong.

## The big idea
Making an answer from data is like making a lemonade:
1. **Collect** the lemons and sugar (get the data).
2. **Clean** them (remove dirt, bad lemons).
3. **Mix** them (combine and calculate).
4. **Taste** it (analyse the answer).
5. **Show** it in a jug (make a chart).
6. **Tell** people what it means (write a report).

If you skip cleaning, one bad lemon spoils the whole jug. That is what happened to the co-op. Their payments were counted twice.

## Words to know
- **Collect**: get the records.
- **Clean**: fix mistakes and remove repeats.
- **Process**: combine records and calculate.
- **Analyse**: answer the question.
- **Visualise**: show it as a picture or chart.
- **Report**: explain what it means and what to do.

## Let's solve it
Trace the co-op problem step by step:
- **Collect**: Payments are written in two places. An app records a payment on the day it happens. A register records it one day later.
- **Clean**: Nobody removed the payments that appear in both places. This is the mistake.
- **Process**: The "late" check used the register date. So some members looked one day late.
- **Analyse and report**: The numbers and the report looked fine. The input was wrong.

The question to ask: "Which step should have caught the double payments?" Answer: cleaning.

## Your turn
Pick a small shop: a bakery, a salon, or Sunita Stores.
1. For each of the six steps, write one place the data comes from.
2. For each step, write one check that would catch a mistake.
3. Which step would be hardest to notice a mistake in? Explain in two sentences.

**Hand in:** a table with three columns: step, where the data comes from, one check.

## Check yourself (trainer's answer key)
1. Cleaning comes before processing. Bad records must be fixed before you add them up.
2. The co-op's report was right in form but wrong in input. That points to cleaning or collection.
3. A check at collection: count the payments in the app and in the register. The two counts should match.

**Answer key note:** For Sunita Stores, good checks include "bill total equals the sum of its items" and "each bill has a date".

## Why this helps your job
Every analyst follows these steps. When a report is wrong, you go back step by step until you find the bad lemon.
