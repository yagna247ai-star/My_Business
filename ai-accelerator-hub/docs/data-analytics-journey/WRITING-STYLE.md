# Writing Style for Day Content

**Rule:** every day must be readable by a 12-year-old. If a 12-year-old cannot follow a sentence, rewrite it.

## The pattern every day follows
1. **The problem**: a real shop or clinic problem, in 3–5 short sentences.
2. **The big idea**: the one concept needed to solve it, explained with an everyday example.
3. **Words to know**: every new word, with a plain meaning.
4. **Let's solve it**: step by step, with the concept applied to the problem.
5. **Your turn**: one task the learner does alone, and what to hand in.
6. **Check yourself**: three questions, with answers for the trainer.
7. **Why this helps your job**: one or two sentences.

## Language rules
- Sentences: 15 words or fewer. One idea per sentence.
- Words: everyday words first. If a technical word is needed, define it the first time, in brackets.
- Examples: use things a 12-year-old knows: a lemonade stand, a school canteen, a cricket scorecard, a mobile recharge, a class test.
- Numbers: small and round, so the maths is easy to check in your head.
- No "obviously", "simply", "just", or "trivially". They make learners feel slow.
- Voice: talk to the learner as "you". Use the shop owner's name, not "the client".
- Code: short. Every line has a comment in plain words.

## Reading level check
- Average sentence: about 12 words.
- No sentence with more than two commas.
- Every acronym (SQL, CSV, KPI) is spelled out the first time.

## Fictional shops (keep the names the same across days)
- **Sunita Stores**: grocery shop, two branches (North, South).
- **Bright Smile Dental Clinic**: small dental clinic.
- **Little Scholars Tuition Centre**: classes for school children.
- **Ganesh Micro-Credit Co-op**: small loans to shop owners.
- **Ravi Textiles**: sells cloth to shops and online.
- **Pinnacle Tools**: a small workshop with 12 workers.

All names, people and numbers are made up.
